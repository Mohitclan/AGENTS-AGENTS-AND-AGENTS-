# Pitchlight: ideal customer and competitors (starter research)

Task T-20261110-20 (J-20261110-08). Starter research for the first campaign: an ideal-customer profile and the top 3 direct competitors. Not a market brief, teardown or channel map. All competitor pages were read on **2026-11-10** through a summarising fetch tool, so re-read exact wording before customer-facing use. Prices are in the vendor's own currency and not converted, so every ₹ against $ line below is not like-for-like: the currencies differ (no exchange rate or date is given here), and so do the limits (Pitchlight Pro: 30 sessions a month; Cluely Pro: no cap stated on the page read; Insight7 Pro: 50 call analyses). Read those lines as price context, not a measured saving. Prices older than 90 days are stale.

Our own facts come only from `knowledge/products/pitchlight.md` (confirmed): Free plan at ₹0 (3 coaching sessions a month), Pro at ₹999 a month (30 sessions), Chrome only, calls transcribed and not recorded, works on Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and Webex, tips visible only to the user. The Preply survey figures are not used.

### What a lead-finder can check with web tools
These are signals visible on public pages. They show that a person sells on calls. They do not show a buying need or intent.
1. A public booking link ("book a discovery call", "book a demo") on the person's own site or profile, which shows client calls happen.
2. A business site that names a founder or consultant and sells services or software through calls (consulting, sales training, coaching, B2B SaaS with "talk to us").
3. India signal: an Indian address, INR prices or an Indian city on the company's own page.
4. Size signal: a team page or About page that shows 1 to 10 people (a solo founder counts).
5. Cadence signal (weaker): public posts or webinars by the person about running calls, demos or client meetings.
6. Exclusions to apply first: direct competitors (never contact, per `knowledge/company.md` line 39 and the 2026-11-04 team decision); anyone under 18; personal email addresses. Companies that sell call recording or call-coaching software are competitors, not leads.
7. Consent screen: skip calls the company's page says are regulated or confidential, until the owners answer the consent question.

Source types to search (hypothesis, not tested here): Peerlist and Product Hunt India profiles, founder and consultant sites, and the company's own pages. A lead task will confirm which of these yield leads.

## Top 3 direct competitors

All three show suggestions on the user's own screen during a live call and sit in the same category as Pitchlight. Chosen because each surfaced in my first two searches, was readable on its own page, and works on the same call apps. The search results themselves are not evidence; each claim below is from the page cited.

### 1. Cluely

- **Positioning:** "Meeting AI that helps during the call, not after" [https://cluely.com/pricing, 2026-11-10].
- **Target customer:** the page names "meetings, homework, sales calls", so a broad and mostly individual audience [same page].
- **Key features:** AI responses, meeting notetaking, uploaded files (3 on Starter), questions about past meetings. Mac download and a mobile app [same page].
- **Pricing (own page, checked 2026-11-10):** Starter free (limited AI responses and notes); Pro $19.99 a month; Pro + Undetectability $149.99 a month ("completely hidden" from screen-sharing software). The page title says "from $11.99/mo", which does not match the $19.99 shown; annual prices are not shown [same page].
- **Gaps in the page:** it does not say whether calls are recorded or transcribed, or whether others on the call are told. Unknown, not "no".
- **Where we win (inferred from the pages):** price shown in INR (₹999) against $19.99 for a paid tier (not like-for-like: different currencies, no conversion, and different usage limits) [our fact sheet; Cluely page]; a stated "transcribed, not recorded" position [fact sheet]; preparation before the call (script, hard questions with answers, cheat sheet) and a recap with follow-up email after, which Cluely's page does not describe as one flow.
- **Where we lose:** Cluely has a desktop app (Mac) and a mobile app, while Pitchlight is Chrome only [fact sheet]. Cluely has a free tier with no stated session cap, only "limited" responses. We have no customer proof yet [fact sheet, proof points: none].

### 2. Convo (itsconvo.com)

- **Positioning:** a real-time AI sales assistant and coach that listens to live calls, shows responses on the user's screen, and syncs follow-ups to the CRM; positioned as a lower-cost alternative to Gong, Clari and Chorus [https://www.itsconvo.com/, 2026-11-10].
- **Target customer:** sales and customer-success teams; consulting and recruiting are mentioned [same page].
- **Key features:** live objection handling, buying-signal detection, answers from company documents and past calls, on-demand help by keyboard shortcut, post-call follow-up drafts and CRM notes, Mac and Windows, Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Webex, Slack [same page]; self-filling playbooks (BANT, SPICED, MEDDPICC), Linux, six interface languages [https://www.itsconvo.com/features, 2026-11-10].
- **Pricing (own page, checked 2026-11-10, per user, annual billing):** Starter $22.49 a month (shown struck down from $29.99); Professional $52.49 a month (from $69.99); Enterprise by quote. No free trial is mentioned. Real-time answers (Live Assistant) are listed only under Professional on one page and "real-time insights" under Starter, an inconsistency on their page [https://www.itsconvo.com/pricing].
- **Gaps in the pages:** the features page says screen-share visibility depends on the operating system, meeting app and sharing mode, and it uses "capture" and "recording" without defining the difference [features page]. No consent statement was found.
- **Where we win:** a free tier and a ₹999 Pro, set beside $22.49 to $52.49 billed annually (our fact sheet; Convo pricing page; not like-for-like: different currencies, no conversion, monthly versus annual billing, different features); built for one person, with no CRM to set up; a stated transcription-only position.
- **Where we lose:** CRM sync, deal-risk and buying-signal tracking, playbooks, Windows and Linux support, and an API. Pitchlight has none of these in its fact sheet [fact sheet features table]. Convo is aimed at teams that have a CRM; a solo consultant may not need it.

### 3. Insight7 Live Assist

- **Positioning:** Insight7 presents an "AI Call Center for AI & Human Agents"; Live Assist gives real-time prompts and next steps during interactions [https://insight7.io/, 2026-11-10]. The Chrome listing: "Real-time AI coaching for sales and service calls on Google Meet and Zoom Web Client" [https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/insight7-live-assist/ggaagpgcbhkiejgdddhaicoglinfpppg, 2026-11-10].
- **Target customer:** customer service, marketing and sales teams in financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, retail and services [insight7.io home]. This is a team and call-centre buyer, a weaker overlap with a solo seller. Included as a direct competitor on the live-coaching feature only.
- **Key features:** coaching cards, objection responses, signal alerts, a live transcript; a flashcard overlay for sales users [Chrome listing]. Works on Google Meet and Zoom Web Client only [Chrome listing].
- **Pricing (own page, checked 2026-11-10, USD):** Pro $99 a month ($990 a year, 1 user, 50 call analyses); Business $299 a month (3 users, 200 analyses); Plus $1,499 a month (20 users); Enterprise on contact. A free plan exists per the FAQ. The pricing page does not say whether Live Assist is in any plan, so what the live feature costs is unknown [https://insight7.io/pricing/].
- **Privacy statement (own listing):** audio goes over an encrypted connection to its transcription service and is not stored after processing [Chrome listing]. No call-consent statement was found.
- **Where we win:** simplicity, and a lower sticker price (₹999 set beside $99 for 1 user; not like-for-like: different currencies, no conversion, and 30 sessions against 50 call analyses), and Teams and Webex support, which the Chrome listing does not list.
- **Where we lose:** a full analytics platform behind it (call and transcript analysis, team features) and the "1000+ businesses" claim on its home page [insight7.io home]. An unverified vendor claim, but we have no proof points at all.

## Research summary

- **Findings:** the live-call coaching category is crowded and mostly priced in USD. Entry paid tiers found: $19.99 (Cluely Pro), $22.49 a month on annual billing (Convo Starter), $99 (Insight7 Pro). Pitchlight's ₹999 and free tier look cheaper at sticker price, but this is a hypothesis, not a finding: the figures are in different currencies with no conversion, and usage limits differ (30 sessions, no stated cap, 50 analyses). None of the pages read states a consent or notice policy for the other people on a call.
- **Sources opened (8 fetches, in three batches):** cluely.com/pricing; itsconvo.com home, features and pricing; the Closr and Insight7 Chrome Web Store listings; insight7.io home and pricing. Re-opening each to verify before publishing is advised; fetched through a summarising tool.
- **Sources not opened:** Pitchlight's own site (taken from the confirmed fact sheet), the Pitchlight guide page, any review site or forum (Cluely complaints, G2, Reddit), Cluely and Convo privacy policies and terms, Closr's own site, Craqly, Tribble, the INR price of any competitor, and any community where buyers gather. So there are no buyer quotes, no review counts and no weaknesses from reviews yet.
- **Confidence:** medium on the competitors' prices and positioning (own pages, dated); low on the ICP triggers, communities and the consent disqualifier (hypotheses, not researched).
- **Open questions for the owners or the next task:**
  1. Do Pitchlight's owners know where transcripts are processed and stored, which AI provider is used, and whether others on the call are told? (Same as the fact sheet's open question.)
  2. Is ₹999 inclusive of GST?
  3. Do we want a review-site pass (G2, Reddit, Chrome Web Store) to get competitor weaknesses in buyers' words? Not done here, by design.
  4. Does the team want Closr or Craqly among the three competitors instead of Insight7, which is a call-centre product?
