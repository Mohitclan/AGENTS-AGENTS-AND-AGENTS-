# Discovery call

meeting-prep builds the page before the call. This skill is how the call runs, and how to learn from it afterwards. A person runs every call; you write the plan and the debrief.

## Before the call

1. **One-line hypothesis** from the lead's evidence: the trigger and the likely pain, tagged `inferred`. It's a guess to test, never something to tell them.
2. **The one outcome you want**: a dated next step (start the free trial on their repo, a demo on their own repo, or a call with the person who decides) or a clear no.

## Running it

1. **Open honestly.** We reached out, so say so, and ask what made them take the call. If they're vague, offer the hypothesis and ask whether it's true for them; if not, offer to keep it short.
2. **Agree the agenda**: their situation, what works and what doesn't, a short look at the product only if it's relevant, and whether there's a next step.
3. **Work down the questions**, one or two open questions each ("what", "how", "walk me through"). Follow-ups beat the script.
   - Situation: how they made their last launch video and docs, and who did it.
   - Problem: what was hard about it.
   - Effect: what happened because of it, in time, money or a missed date.
   - Payoff: what fixing it would change, in their words.
   - Next launch: what and when (this is segment 1's timing test).
4. **Qualify live on four yes-or-no tests**: a specific pain; an effect they can name; a dated need within about six months; they decide, or can take us to who does. Fewer than four: close kindly, give an honest pointer ("come back when the launch has a date"), and record their words on the lead.
5. **Pitch only when they ask or agree**, for two or three minutes, from confirmed facts (claim-check). Then ask whether it solves the problem they described, and wait.
6. **Agree one next step before the call ends.** No next step means no opportunity yet.

## Deflections

| They say | You do |
|---|---|
| "Just send me some information" | Ask what exactly would help, then send only that |
| "What does it cost?" | Answer from the fact sheet. Our prices are public; dodging is dishonest |
| "We're fine as we are" | Ask what made them take the call |
| "Just exploring" | Ask what changed; if nothing, close politely |

**No-show:** after 5-10 minutes, one short note with an easy way out. After three reschedules, offer the recorded demo instead of another call.

## After the call: the debrief

1. **No notes, no debrief.** Work only from the caller's notes, or a transcript they agreed to. Never from the meeting title.
2. **Plan against reality.** Mark each goal and question from the meeting-prep page as done, partly done or missed, quoting their words (`prospect_said`).
3. **Read signals from their words only**, never from tone.
   - Strong: they asked how setup works, asked for something to share with a colleague, or asked what the first weeks look like.
   - Weak: "interesting", "let me think", an unspecific "send info".
   - Red flags: "we'll discuss internally" with no date, repeated rescheduling, only one person engaged on a group call.
4. **Every call ends in a next step or a disqualification.** If it didn't, say so first.
5. **Hand off.** New objections go to the objection register in their exact words. Questions we had no content for go to reply-triage's content-gap list. A deal going quiet goes to stalled-deal.
6. **Patterns.** After about ten debriefs, a script counts the most-missed item (pitching early, skipping price, vague next steps) and you give the caller one coaching line.

## Never

- Invent benchmarks ("teams like yours pay...") or work out their costs from an assumed hourly rate. Use only figures they gave.
- Record a call without their spoken yes.
- Commit to discounts, extensions or terms beyond the fact sheet: mark them `[NEEDS APPROVAL]`.
- Note personal details beyond their professional role.
