# Press pitch

marketing.md promises journalist and newsletter pitches. Every pitch is a draft; a person sends it.

1. **Is PR worth it now?** It needs all three:
   - a real story: data we own, a founder's sharp opinion, a confirmed milestone, or a customer before-and-after with a proof-library permission;
   - founder time;
   - a page for readers to land on.

   "We exist" before launch goes on the not-yet list (early-stage-guardrails).
2. **Pick one angle**: the founding story; small against large; or a broken way of doing things that the product fixes (never a named competitor). The product is the evidence, not the story.
3. **A media list of 20-40 at most, from public pages.** For each journalist, newsletter or podcast, record:
   - the last five pieces you read, with URLs and dates;
   - whether the beat matches;
   - whether the outlet reaches our buyers;
   - a published business contact, or the outlet's pitch form;
   - any "no PR pitches" note, which you respect.

   Score beat ×3, buyer fit ×2, recency ×1. Reach with no source is "unknown".
4. **The pitch, under 150 words**:
   - a subject that predicts the headline;
   - a first line tied to their recent piece;
   - the story in one line;
   - why their readers care;
   - what's new, from confirmed facts only;
   - an offer (an interview, a demo, data);
   - one ask.
5. **Quality bar**:
   - the beat is checked;
   - there's a real hook;
   - the journalist could write the story from the email alone;
   - one clear ask;
   - lint-copy is clean.
6. **Press page checklist**, from confirmed facts: a description, founder bios (each founder approves their own), logos, screenshots, a contact.
7. **Decide the outcome before drafting**: placements, sign-ups through a tagged link, sales calls that mention the coverage. No "ad value equivalent".

## Never

- Guess a journalist's email address. Published professional contacts only.
- Offer an exclusive or an embargo without approval: `[NEEDS APPROVAL]`.
- Answer media-request services (HARO and similar). That's sending, so a person does it.
- Use paid media databases or SEO tools.
