# Event outreach

1. **Pick events from the channel map**, or ones a person names. Only events a team member will attend, or that publish a speaker or demo lineup. Look about 180 days ahead and 90 days back.
2. **Targets from public pages only**: speaker lists, demo-day lineups, sponsor and exhibitor pages, workshop hosts. The talk or demo slot, with its date, is the evidence (`published`). Never use attendee or RSVP lists, even if you can see them, or event apps behind a login. Every target still goes through `node scripts/leads.mjs` and the lead gate.
3. **Tier the list.**
   - Must-meet, 5-10: existing leads or strong fits.
   - Should-meet, 15-25.
   - Everyone else, only if time allows.
4. **One to three weeks before**, draft a short note to each must-meet whose contact is published on their own site. It names the event and their talk, says our founder will be there, and makes one small ask. The person attending fills in times and places.
5. **A one-page brief for the person attending:**
   - a 30-second introduction that ends in a question, not a pitch;
   - three questions: what's hard about launching, how they do it today, and whether to keep talking after the event;
   - a notes format: the pain in their words, how they do it now, the next step, and how warm they are (hot, warm or cool).
6. **Same-day follow-ups** from those notes, tagged `prospect_said`:
   - Hot: the agreed next step.
   - Warm: one useful resource we already have.
   - Cool: a short thank-you.

   After that, cold-email's follow-up limits apply.
7. **If our founder speaks or demos**, follow-ups use the talk as the shared context.

## Never

- Note family, hobbies or anything personal; professional context only.
- Add anyone to a newsletter or list without their consent.
- Count an event's audience as buyers.
- Spend money: the event budget is zero unless a person decides otherwise.

## How to tell it helped

Per event, count as X of N: targets, notes drafted and sent, conversations logged, follow-ups, replies and trials.
