# Positioning

## Build the evidence ledger first

One row per claim you want to make:

| Segment | Alternative they use today | Our capability | Value to them | Proof | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Start from the alternative, not the feature. Doing nothing, a freelancer or a spreadsheet all count. A row with no proof stays a hypothesis, and hypothesis positioning isn't final copy.

## Then write, in order

1. **Who it's for,** narrow enough to exclude someone.
2. **The problem,** in their words, from the language bank.
3. **The alternative,** and where it falls short.
4. **Why us,** the one capability they can't easily get elsewhere.
5. **Proof.**
6. **A one-line pitch and three key messages,** each with its proof.

## Agreement check

Before anyone uses it, check that:

1. A stranger can say who it's for after five seconds.
2. Each message has proof behind it.
3. It's true today, not on the roadmap.
4. A competitor couldn't paste it onto their own site.
5. The buyer's own words appear.
6. Sales, docs and the website say the same thing.
7. Someone in the segment has reacted to it, or it's marked assumed.

## Stage guardrail

If nobody outside the team has validated it, say so at the top: "assumed, test with: ___". Name the cheapest test.
