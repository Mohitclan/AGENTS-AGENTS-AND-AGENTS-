# Rival teardown

One competitor per run. Feeds knowledge/competitors/ and competitive-battlecard; the battlecard rules win where they differ (no knocking rivals, no stated hypotheses as facts).

1. **Cap the depth** at about 12 searches and 12 page reads. Stop a topic when two reads add nothing new.
2. **News from the last 90 days**: shutdowns, acquisitions, price changes, launches. Date each item, and say what it opens up for us. Name exactly who bought what.
3. **Their biggest recent launch**, broken into hook, setup, reveal, proof and call to action. Say how you read it: the video itself, a transcript only, or the page text only.
4. **What a buyer like ours would pay.** Work out what a segment-1 founder making one launch video a month would pay, from the rival's own dated pricing page, in its own currency. Note hidden costs, and which tier holds the features that matter. Never write "cheaper than" without both prices in one currency at a dated rate.
5. **Their headline, word for word**: who it's for and the lead claims, quoted from their page.
6. **Buyers' voice.** At least one exact quote of praise and one complaint, with links, from more than one site where you can. Name the sites, how many reviews you read on each, and any bias in a site. Never skip the praise: it's why their customers stay.
7. **Hiring, customers and search.**
   - Open roles, grouped by team.
   - Named customers, marked confirmed, likely or a guess.
   - Who ranks for "<rival> alternative".
8. **"Couldn't read" isn't "none found".** Pages behind a login or a browser go on a "needs a person" list with the exact URL.
9. **Snapshot for next time.** Append to the competitor file's Changes section, each item dated: entry price, the typical-buyer cost, the headline, open roles, review counts and launches.

## Never

- Estimate their ad spend, or call any ad their "best".
- Use paid ad libraries or review tools.
- Quote a complaint without its link and date.
