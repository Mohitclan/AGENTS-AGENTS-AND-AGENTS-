# Proof library

## Keep a row for every proof item

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| What | The exact quote or number |
| Source | Where it came from, with a date |
| Who | Named person and role, or anonymised |
| Permission | none, internal only, anonymised, public, or withdrawn |
| Applies to | Which product, plan and segment |
| Expires | When to recheck it |

No row, no use. If permission is unclear, it's `none`.

## Rules

1. Numbers and quotes are frozen. Don't round, combine or tidy them.
2. Story sentences are either [S] sourced or [C] connective. Connective sentences join the sourced ones; they may not add events, results or feelings.
3. An unusually good result must say what's typical.
4. Say what the number is out of. "Saved 40 hours" needs "per month, across 3 people".
5. Keep a withdrawn list. Withdrawn proof comes out of every live document.
6. Sample or demo data is never proof, even if it looks real.

## With no proof yet

Don't write around the gap. Use what is true: what the product does, how it works, a free trial, a real output they can see. Never write "trusted by", "customers love" or "teams everywhere" without a row behind it.

## Choosing proof

Match the buyer's fear: a security worry needs a security fact, not a growth story. One specific proof beats three vague ones.
