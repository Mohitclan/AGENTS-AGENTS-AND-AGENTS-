# Market sizing

Numbers are where invented facts creep in. Every input is sourced or labelled "an assumption, because...".

1. **Which market.** List the 2-3 categories the product could be filed under, using the words buyers use (search phrases, directory categories, community names). Recommend one, and say what changes if another is picked: rivals, buyers and prices. Name the do-nothing options too: a screen recorder, an agency or freelancer, doing it in-house, or no video at all. Positioning stays marketing's call; this is evidence for it.
2. **Top-down, as context only.** Two or three published estimates, each with publisher, year, figure and what it counts, quoted as theirs. Say why they differ. Never present one as our market.
3. **Bottom-up reachable market, per segment**: likely buyers × yearly price.
   - **Count in the right unit**: companies that could buy, from directories, launch lists, registries or job-post counts. Never count community members, followers or attendees as buyers.
   - **Show the arithmetic**, one line per segment, and name the input that matters most. If that input is an assumption, call the total an order of magnitude.
4. **Reachable isn't winnable.** Never multiply a count by a trial or conversion benchmark from someone else. A share-we-could-win line needs our own data; with no sales yet, say what data would size it.
5. **Price norms, grouped by what the money buys**:
   - self-serve video tools;
   - docs-from-code tools;
   - agencies and freelancers;
   - an in-house hire.

   Give one norm per group, each price dated from its own page. Never average across groups.
6. **Label demand by how it's known**: measured, proxy (say of what), a known pattern that isn't measured, or unknown. List dates in the next 90 days that matter: launch-venue cycles, events, budget seasons.
7. **Leave a baseline for next time**: each count, price norm and date, with its source and the date you read it, so the next brief can show what changed.

## Never

- Present a single number without its inputs.
- Use a paid data source. If a free source can't be read, write "couldn't read" and say what a person could fetch.
- Fill a gap from memory. A missing input stays labelled as missing.
