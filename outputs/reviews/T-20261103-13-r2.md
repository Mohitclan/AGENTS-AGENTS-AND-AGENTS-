## Rulings on the two points

**1. Is "start the free trial" plus a feedback ask one call to action or two?**

It is one call to action. The conversion action is "start the free trial", in the sheet's exact words (line 29), and it is the same in all three comments (lines 70, 98, 130). The feedback ask is not a second conversion, and it competes with nothing: it asks for a reply in the thread the reader is already reading, which is what the venues tell makers to do. I re-opened both pages today:
- **Peerlist:** "Ask for support by adding feedback by commenting".
- **Product Hunt:** "ask them to visit and comment".

On Product Hunt, "then" puts the two in order.

LaunchPad India is weaker. Its Terms, re-opened today, describe no comments on listings at all. Line 94 already makes that comment conditional ("use only if the form offers one"), so the ask cannot appear where there is nowhere to reply. Accepted on all three. For the self-review skill, this is the case my memory notes: a comment ask the venue encourages is fine as an addition to the exact CTA, provided the file says so. Line 201 does say so.

**2. Is "one-pager" in the Product Hunt opener (line 112) a fair hook?**

It is not deceptive, but it is a stretch, and it needs a one-word change (note 1, Medium).

What supports it: the product's own blog positions the sales PDF as the replacement for a one-pager:
- "Why does it beat a hand-written one-pager?" (`repos/motionrepo/frontend/content/blog/posts-documents.ts:72-83`)
- "Stop rewriting the one-pager" (line 233)
- send it "Whenever someone asks for 'a one-pager'" (line 474)

So the hook follows how MotionRepo markets itself.

Why it still stretches:
1. The fact sheet says "a sales PDF" (line 32), never "one-pager", and no claims row covers it.
2. The blog is not on the sheet and is not a confirmed source.
3. The sales PDF is not one page. The blog lists eight parts, from cover to next steps (`posts-documents.ts:40-49`), and calls the use-cases example "the first page of the sales PDF" (line 51).
4. The homepage, re-opened today, does not use the word.

The question names a gap, and the next sentence answers it with "a sales PDF", so a reader takes the two to be the same. That joins two facts into a new claim (claim-check rule 6; main memory, 2026-10-30). Calling it a hook in line 158 does not change what the reader infers. This is not an auto-fail: the feature exists, and nothing is invented about it. It does cost a point under Accurate.
